Start with the retail decision

Visitor patterns, tenant performance and customer feedback provide a basis for decisions about a shopping centre’s future. Read alongside catchment research and the destination’s positioning strategy, these insights can inform tenant mix, space allocation and the balance of retail, dining and entertainment. A clear commercial brief gives any proposed AI application a defined purpose within those decisions.

Our work in retail planning, research and positioning provides a commercial basis for these discussions. For Retail AI, the starting point is the property decision and the evidence needed to support it.

  • Identify the decision the analysis should inform
  • Establish which information is available and fit for that purpose
  • Connect findings with practical choices for the destination
  • Set a clear basis for reviewing any proposed technology

When to explore Retail AI

A planned refurbishment, a change in trading patterns or an upcoming leasing cycle can raise questions that existing reports leave unresolved. These are useful moments to examine whether a different analytical approach could support the next decision.

Start with a defined question for your property:

  • Where does customer activity differ from the intended retail offer?
  • Which gaps in the tenant mix warrant further research?
  • What evidence would support a change in zoning or circulation?
  • Which decisions need better information before funds are committed?

Connect retail advice with technology decisions

Retail teams and technology providers bring different expertise to the same brief. Commercial objectives, available data and technical requirements need to be considered together. The property team should be able to explain how a proposed output would change a planning, leasing or operating decision.

Responsibilities need to be agreed for each appointment. A retail advisory brief should state who interprets the findings, who supplies the technology and who manages the data.

Define the scope around the commercial brief

A useful brief sets out the question, the evidence required and the form of the recommendation. Four areas can help frame the discussion about an appropriate Retail AI scope.

01 Customer and market questions

Define the visitor groups, catchment changes and customer needs that the assessment should examine. Consumer research and competitor studies provide context for interpreting behavioural patterns. The aim is to connect any new insight with a positioning strategy that suits the destination.

02 Tenant mix and anchor strategy

Identify where category performance or customer demand deserves closer review. This can frame questions about the tenant mix, anchor strategy and the suitability of new-to-market brands. Recommendations still need to account for retailer requirements, unit availability and the wider leasing brief.

03 Zoning and the retail programme

Set out how findings would inform broad zoning, adjacencies or the retail programme. A design review can then consider the physical implications alongside the architect. The brief should explain which choices remain open and which are constrained by the existing building.

04 Measures and next steps

Agree how the team would judge whether an assessment has helped. Relevant measures might concern the quality of a decision, the time needed to prepare an analysis or a defined property outcome. Baselines, limitations and follow-up responsibilities should be clear from the outset.

A practical route from question to decision

The right sequence depends on the property and the proposed scope. A focused assessment can be structured around four stages.

  1. 01 Define the decision

    Set out the commercial objective, the decision owner and the timing. Agree which question matters most and what would make the findings useful to the team.

  2. 02 Review the evidence

    Establish what information exists, the period it covers and where gaps remain. Check whether the available evidence is suitable for the question before selecting an analytical approach.

  3. 03 Assess the options

    Consider the role of research, analysis and any proposed AI application. Relate the findings to the positioning, tenant mix or planning choices under review, with assumptions made clear.

  4. 04 Agree the next action

    Translate the assessment into a decision or a defined next step. Record what needs further testing and who will take it forward within the agreed appointment.

Keep the destination in view

An office worker buying lunch and a family spending an afternoon use the same centre differently. Their needs should inform the balance of retail, dining and entertainment (RDE), the role of anchors and the connections between uses.

Any analysis needs that property context. A rise in visits may prompt further questions about spend, repeat visits or how activity is distributed across the centre. Useful insight gives the team a clearer question to act on.

Questions about Retail AI

What does AI-driven shopping mall consultancy mean?

It describes the use of AI within advice on shopping centre decisions. A clear brief connects the proposed application with a commercial question, suitable data and a practical output. The scope needs to state what the advisor and any technology provider will each deliver.

How does Retail AI relate to retail technology consulting?

Retail technology consulting is a broader term that can cover systems, software and implementation. This page focuses on the retail property questions behind an AI brief, including customer demand, positioning and planning. Technical delivery responsibilities need to be agreed separately.

What data should a landlord bring to the discussion?

Begin with an outline of the information already available, such as visitor counts, tenant sales summaries, customer research or leasing records. Its relevance depends on coverage, consistency and the decision being considered. Data access and use permissions should be established before any transfer.

Can AI help inform tenant mix decisions?

Analysis may identify patterns worth investigating, subject to the data and methods used. Tenant mix decisions still require a view of customer demand, positioning, unit constraints and retailer interest. A recommendation should explain how those considerations support the proposed change.

Does a Retail AI enquiry include leasing execution?

Leasing Strategy & Execution is a separate service. Husband Retail undertakes appointments as exclusive leasing partner where agreed under the mandate. Tenant introductions, negotiations and lease execution need their own defined responsibilities and scope.

Can we discuss an existing shopping centre?

Yes. Tell us about the centre, the question you are trying to resolve and the information currently available. That gives the discussion a practical starting point for considering the relevant retail advisory scope and any further expertise needed.
