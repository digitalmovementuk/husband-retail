The Landmark, Hong Kong

Market intelligence grounded in the decisions ahead

A retail destination needs a clear view of who it will serve and why those customers will visit. Market evidence helps establish that view before the team commits to a concept, mix or layout. Our research brings customer demand, competition and retailer requirements into the same discussion, giving retail planning a commercial basis.

The findings help the project team assess:

  • Which customers and occasions the destination can serve
  • Where competing destinations leave room for a different offer
  • What scale and mix the market may support
  • How retailer requirements should inform the proposed space

When to bring research into your retail plans

Research is useful when the next property decision depends on assumptions that need testing. A new development may need evidence of demand. An operating destination may need a clearer view of changing customers, competition or the relevance of its current offer.

Typical questions include:

  • Is there demand for the proposed concept and retail scale?
  • Which customer groups should the positioning strategy address?
  • Could the tenant mix better reflect how people use the destination?
  • Which brands and operators could fit the market opportunity?

Connecting market evidence with retail planning

Customer demand and retailer requirements shape the same property decisions. We bring both perspectives into the assessment so the team can connect the market opportunity with practical choices about the destination. The research scope reflects the project stage and the questions still open.

What our retail market research covers

Our project work draws on four areas of research. The brief determines which are needed and how the findings feed into development, positioning or leasing advice.

01 Market and demand analysis

We examine the market conditions and demand drivers relevant to the destination. Where the scope includes footfall and sales projections, these help assess supportable retail scale. The analysis gives the team a basis for testing the size and ambition of the proposed offer.

02 Consumer research

Consumer research examines the people the destination could serve, their behaviour and the needs behind a visit. These findings inform the retail proposition and the balance of uses, helping the team relate its plans to the customers it intends to attract.

03 Competitor studies and benchmarks

We assess competing destinations and relevant project benchmarks to identify the choices already available to customers. This informs positioning strategy and the potential for a distinct offer. Benchmarks provide context for the brief, with their relevance considered against the local market.

04 Brand interviews and retailer insight

Brand interviews bring retailer intentions and requirements into the assessment. The findings can inform brand mix, shop sizes, adjacencies and technical provisions. They help the team assess potential new-to-market brands and the implications of accommodating them within the proposed scheme.

Together, these inputs connect market opportunity with decisions the project team can act on.

Research applied in project work

The following projects show how research has informed broader retail consultancy appointments.

Taipei Twin Towers, Taiwan

For Taipei Twin Towers Limited, our work included macro-economic and demand analysis, competition analysis and consumer dynamics research. Footfall and sales projections informed supportable scale. Transport-hub retail benchmarks contributed to positioning, trade mix and zoning recommendations, followed by a detailed design brief for the interior architect.

Sanya Central Business District, China

For Gree Real Estate, market analysis of Hainan focused on competition, supported by consumer, retailer and competitor studies. The work informed positioning strategy, concepts and brand mixes for individual areas, with anchor strategy, broad zoning and tenant recommendations developed for the destination.

Haitang Bay theme-park retail, Sanya

For Keyestone Group, brand interviews on Hainan informed the retail component of an integrated theme-park development. The consultancy covered zoning aligned with the theme-park positioning, shop sizes and adjacencies, and the implications for layouts, technical provisions and operations. Design reviews carried those requirements into discussions with the architects.

From the research brief to retail recommendations

The scope starts with the decisions the client needs to make. Research can then inform the relevant parts of the retail strategy and programme.

  1. 01 Define the questions

    Identify the property objectives, project stage and assumptions that need examination. Agree which decisions the research should support and the depth of work required.

  2. 02 Examine the market

    Bring together the agreed market analysis, consumer research, competitor studies and retailer input. Assess what these findings mean for the proposed destination.

  3. 03 Develop the implications

    Translate the evidence into recommendations for positioning, retail scale and mix. Connect those recommendations with the planning or commercial questions in the brief.

  4. 04 Inform the next stage

    Use the findings to guide the agreed development, repositioning or leasing work. The wider appointment determines the planning detail and support required.

Research around how people use the destination

Residents, office workers, tourists and people passing through a transport hub can bring different reasons to visit. Their needs influence the balance of retail, dining and entertainment (RDE), the role of anchors and the connections between uses.

Retail market research gives those choices a clearer basis. For mixed-use schemes, it helps the team consider how the retail offer relates to neighbouring offices, homes, hotels and public spaces.

Questions about retail market research

What does retail market research cover?

The scope can include market and demand analysis, consumer research, competitor studies, benchmarks and brand interviews. Each input addresses a different part of the brief. Together, they inform the decisions about positioning, scale and mix that the client needs to make.

Can research inform a shopping mall feasibility study?

Yes. Market and consumer evidence can help assess demand and supportable retail scale. Footfall and sales projections may form part of the scope, as demonstrated at Taipei Twin Towers. Projections remain estimates based on the assumptions used for the study.

Can you research an existing retail destination?

Yes. Research can inform a review of an operating destination and its offer. At Covent Garden, market research formed part of a broader appointment covering positioning strategy, street-level layout and zoning, retail mix and the marketing plan.

How do brand interviews inform the plans?

Brand interviews help establish retailer intentions and requirements. At Haitang Bay, they formed part of consultancy covering zoning, shop sizes, adjacencies and design implications. Retailer input helps test the proposed offer; interest expressed during research does not establish a lease commitment.

What is the difference between research and a retail market report?

A retail market report can provide an overview of a market or sector. Research for a property appointment addresses the questions behind that project, connecting market evidence with its proposed customers, positioning and retail offer. The brief determines the required outputs and level of detail.

Does research include leasing or design services?

Research can inform both. Development consultancy takes the findings into retail planning and design review. Leasing Strategy & Execution covers tenant approaches and negotiations under an agreed mandate, which may include appointment as exclusive leasing partner. The scope of each service is agreed with the client.
