Our story

Making malls that make customers smile. Since 1998.

We opened in Hong Kong on 1 April 1998 with one client and one discipline. The client was Hongkong Land, and for the first couple of years it was our only one. The discipline was marketing and branding: we handled all the marketing of its Landmark portfolio in Central, down to buying the advertising space.

Planning came next, because deciding what a centre is to be, and which shops and restaurants belong in it, is an extension of its marketing. We could picture the stores and brands a project needed, so we took on positioning, zoning and tenant mix, then layout, design review and design development with the architects, and we hired surveyors to read the floor plans and handle the technical side.

Leasing took longer. We had the relationships with brands and had planned many malls, yet it was two years before we agreed to lease what we planned. Our first significant mandate was Elements in Hong Kong, for MTR Corporation, where only brands not yet in the city could be approached. Representing tenants and brands in new markets followed.

That history explains how we are built. The firm was founded from the landlord's side, by a former head of marketing at Swire Properties, and many of our team have worked for well-known landlords; most leasing agents have worked only as agents. Large agencies are organised by country or regional profit centre. We are one team working across borders, so what opens in the United States, Europe and the UK informs our work in Asia. We also know what tenants need: the store sizes, and the adjacencies, a sensitive matter for luxury brands especially. Luxury is only part of our work.

Every project starts from one question: what will delight the customers of that particular place. The answer has three layers: their essentials, their aspirations, and what we add from the region and the world, which is often an experiment. In planning we hold to a few rules: open sight lines, an escalator or lift always in view, arcades neither compressed nor remote, and the dumbbell layout of two atria linked by three floors. We are not designers. We give owners and their architects the market inputs a design needs, above all the store sizes the intended tenants require.

Once a centre trades, we read it through its sales. Turnover rent and monthly sales reports show each category's sales per square foot, so strong tenants move up and weak ones make way. Today we do the same work at a larger scale, through six services: retail development consultancy, positioning and repositioning, retail mix planning, leasing strategy and execution, marketing and placemaking, and retail brand advisory.

The founder

Paul Husband spent about ten years on Pacific Place and the Swire portfolio before founding Husband Retail in 1998.

Paul Husband has been in Hong Kong since 1985. Dentsu hired him to launch Pacific Place, and for the first three years he worked from Swire Properties' offices, building the team and taking the centre through its soft opening and its grand opening.

Swire then hired him as head of marketing for Pacific Place and two other malls in the same division; in all he spent about ten years on the Swire portfolio. The marketing was resourced to match, with an 18-person team and an annual budget of several million US dollars.

Pacific Place was planned as a destination brand, and the case for planning it that way rested on spending per visit: Swire's research found that visitors who came for the experience spent about three times as much per visit as purpose shoppers. For several years the centre was the second most productive mall in the world, after the Forum Shops in Las Vegas.

He left with a knowledge of sales productivity across every category, hundreds of relationships and the trust of brands. He secured Hongkong Land as a client before he resigned, and founded Husband Retail in Hong Kong on 1 April 1998. He has led the firm since, as Founder and Managing Partner.

Who we work with

We act for owners, developers and investors, and for one brand at a time.

Our clients are the parties that build, own or buy a retail destination. We also act for brands, on narrow terms.

Developers

A developer planning a new centre has to decide whom it is for, how it is laid out and zoned, and which trades and tenants it needs. We define the positioning, plan the retail mix, work through design review and design development with the architects, run the leasing and plan the marketing. Elements for MTR Corporation, Galaxy Macau Phase 2 for Galaxy Entertainment and Merdeka 118 for PNB were appointments of that kind.

Investors and owners

An investor buying or holding a centre needs to know how each part of it trades and what should change. We read turnover rent and monthly sales reports category by category, define the repositioning and plan a leasing strategy in which strong tenants move up and weak ones make way. Three on the Bund for House of Three Ltd and Covent Garden for Capco were repositioning appointments.

Retailers and brands

A brand entering a new market has to choose the centre, the size of the store and its neighbours. Store sizes and adjacencies are what we plan on the landlord's side, so we know how to judge them for a tenant. We have represented Ralph Lauren, Armani Group and others. The limits are firm: one retail brand at a time, market entry only, up to the signed lease. What the store then sells, and how, is the operator's business; we do not consult on retailing itself.
