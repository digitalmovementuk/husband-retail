Retail property advice from people who focus on retail.

General property firms cover many types of property. Our work centres on retail space, and these are the four ways we help.

Owners and investors

Advice for owners and investors

Owners and investors come to us when they need a clear view on a retail property: how it is performing, where its potential lies and what it would take to unlock it. We look at the tenant mix, rents, footfall and the wider market around the building.

Our advice is written for decision makers, with clear options and the reasoning behind each one.

Developers

Retail input for developers

For new developments, we bring retail thinking into the project early: the concept, the amount and type of retail space, the position of anchors and how shoppers will move through the building.

This helps developers avoid costly redesigns later and gives the leasing team a scheme that tenants want to be part of.

Research and data

Decisions backed by numbers

Good advice needs good evidence. We use catchment, shopper, rent and competitor data to test assumptions before they become decisions, and we explain the findings in plain language.

Where data is missing, we say so and suggest how to fill the gap.

Leasing

From advice to signed leases

Leasing and tenant representation have been part of our work for years, so our advice stays close to what tenants will actually sign. Every recommendation comes with a practical route to put it in place.

Clients can use us for advice only, or ask the same team to carry the plan through to agency leasing.

Questions

Frequently asked questions

What do retail property consultants do for owners and developers?

Retail property consultants advise owners, developers and investors on retail space: what to build or change, which tenants to target, what rents to expect and how to lease it. The work can range from a single question about one building to a full strategy for a shopping mall.

How is a retail property consultant different from a general property agent?

A general agent usually covers many types of property and focuses on transactions. A retail property consultant focuses on how retail space performs: tenant mix, shopper behaviour, circulation and leasing strategy. That focus matters most for malls and larger retail schemes.

Which kinds of clients do your retail property consultants advise?

We advise retail landlords, mall owners, developers and investors, and we also act for retailers and brands looking for space. Most of our work is in Hong Kong, with projects across Asia Pacific and the Middle East over more than two decades.

Can you review an existing mall or retail podium for us?

Yes. We can review an existing mall or retail podium and set out where it stands today: tenant mix, vacancy, rents, footfall and the competition nearby. The review ends with practical options and the likely effect of each one. [CONFIRM: review format]

How much does retail real estate consulting usually cost?

It depends on the scope and the size of the property. A focused review costs less than a full strategy for a new development. We agree the scope and fee basis in writing before work begins. [CONFIRM: fee basis]
