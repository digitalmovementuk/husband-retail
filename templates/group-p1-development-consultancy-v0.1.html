Good retail starts long before the first tenant signs.

Most of the value in a retail project is set in the early decisions. These four stages are where our strategy work makes the biggest difference.

Concept

Start with the right concept

Before anyone draws a floor plan, the project needs a clear answer to a simple question: who is this place for, and why would they come back? We look at the catchment, the competition and the site to shape a retail concept that fits the location.

That concept then guides every later decision, from unit sizes and anchor positions to the brands the leasing team approaches first.

Feasibility

Test the numbers early

A shopping mall feasibility study checks whether the concept works in practice: how much retail the area can support, what rents are achievable and how the project might be phased. It gives owners and investors a sound basis for the go or no-go decision.

Where the numbers do not work, we say so early, while the design can still change at little cost.

Planning

Plans that work for retail

Retail masterplanning and mall circulation planning decide how people move through a project: entrances, vertical links, sightlines and the position of anchors. Small changes on the plan can make the difference between a busy upper floor and an empty one.

We work alongside your architects to keep the plan practical for tenants, shoppers and the leasing team, including on transit-oriented developments where station flows shape the whole scheme.

Leasing plan

A strategy that ends in signed leases

Strategy pays off when it leads to the right tenants. Leasing has been part of our work since 1998, so every strategy we write ends with a practical leasing plan: target brands, zoning and a timeline.

If you want us to, the same team can then carry that plan through to agency leasing, so nothing gets lost between advice and delivery.

Questions

Frequently asked questions

What is retail strategy consulting and what does it include?

Retail strategy consulting sets the direction for a retail project or an existing mall: what it offers, who it serves, which tenants it needs and how it will be leased. It brings together concept, feasibility, planning and leasing advice, so owners can decide based on one clear plan.

When should a developer bring in a retail strategy consultant?

As early as possible, ideally before the design is fixed. At that stage, decisions on unit sizes, circulation and anchor positions are still cheap to change. Retail advice later in the project is still useful, but some options will already be closed.

What does a shopping mall feasibility study usually cover?

A feasibility study usually covers the catchment and its spending power, competing centres, achievable rents, the right amount and mix of retail space, and possible phasing. The aim is a clear, evidence-based answer on whether and how the project should go ahead. [CONFIRM: standard study contents]

Do you work on transit-oriented developments in Hong Kong?

Yes. Transit-oriented developments are one of our practice areas. Station flows, entrances and links to the surrounding streets shape how the retail parts of these projects perform, so we plan the tenant mix and circulation around them.

Do you only work on retail projects in Hong Kong?

Our base is in Hong Kong, where the firm was founded in 1998. Over more than two decades we have worked on more than 100 retail projects across Asia Pacific and the Middle East, so we can support projects outside Hong Kong as well.
