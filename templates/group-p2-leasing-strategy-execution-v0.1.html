Leasing that starts with the property, then finds the tenants.

Agency leasing works best when the landlord, the plan and the tenants line up. These are the four parts of how we approach it.

Leasing plan

A plan before the first viewing

Every leasing brief starts with the space itself: floor plates, frontage, circulation and the people who live and work nearby. From there we set out which trades belong on which level, which brands to approach first and what rent each zone can realistically carry.

The result is one leasing plan your board can read in a few minutes and check month by month, instead of a pile of enquiries that arrive in no particular order.

Tenant mix

The right tenants, in the right order

A mall is judged by its mix. Anchors and key brands set the tone, specialty retail fills the journey between them, and food and beverage keeps people on site for longer. We plan that sequence so each signing makes the next one easier.

Where a unit stays vacant, we look at why before marketing it again: the size, the position, the rent or the neighbouring tenants.

Street and mall retail

From high street to shopping mall

Our leasing work covers shopping malls, retail podiums, street shops and transit-oriented developments. Each has its own tenants and its own rhythm, and Hong Kong rents can change sharply from one block to the next.

Because we also act for retailers and brands on tenant representation, we know what tenants look for when they compare one location with another.

Lease-up and renewals

Lease-up that holds

The work continues after a tenant signs. We handle negotiations and heads of terms, then stay involved through fit-out, opening, renewals and, where needed, lease restructuring.

Landlords receive regular progress reports covering enquiries, offers, signings and vacancy, so there are no surprises at the next board meeting. [CONFIRM: reporting rhythm]

Questions

Frequently asked questions

What does an agency leasing team do for a landlord?

An agency leasing team markets your retail space, finds and qualifies tenants, negotiates terms and manages the lease-up on your behalf. For a mall, that also means planning the tenant mix so that each new brand supports the others and the centre as a whole.

How long does it take to lease up a new shopping mall in Hong Kong?

It depends on the size of the mall, its location and how early leasing starts. We usually begin before construction is finished, so anchors and key tenants are signed well ahead of opening. We agree a realistic timeline with you at the start. [CONFIRM: typical timeline range]

Do you also represent tenants and retail brands looking for space?

Yes. Alongside landlord work, we act for retailers and brands looking for space in Hong Kong, including brands entering the market from overseas. Where both sides could be involved in one deal, we agree with you upfront how this is handled. [CONFIRM: conflict policy]

Which types of retail property does your leasing team handle?

We lease shopping malls, retail podiums in mixed-use buildings, street shops, outlet centres and the retail parts of transit-oriented developments. Food and beverage, luxury and lifestyle units are all part of our day-to-day leasing work.

How are agency leasing fees for a mall or retail podium structured?

Fees depend on the scope: a full lease-up of a new mall is different from leasing a few vacant units. We set out the fee basis in writing before work begins, so you know what you pay and when. [CONFIRM: fee basis]
