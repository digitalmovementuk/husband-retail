Research that answers the question you actually have.

Good retail research starts with the decision, then gathers the evidence for it. These are the four areas we usually cover.

Catchment

Who lives and works nearby

Every retail location depends on the people within reach of it. We map the catchment around a site: residents, office workers, visitors and the transport links that bring them in, along with their spending power.

This shows how much retail an area can support and which kinds of shops and restaurants it needs.

Shoppers

How people shop the area

Numbers on a map tell only part of the story. Shopper research looks at how people actually use an area: where they go, how often, how long they stay and what they would like to find that is missing today.

Combined with footfall data, this gives a grounded view of demand. [CONFIRM: survey methods used]

Rents and competition

What the market pays

We look at competing centres and shopping streets nearby: their size, tenant mix, rents and vacancy. That shows where a project can stand out and what rent levels are realistic for different types of space.

For brands, the same work helps compare locations before committing to a lease.

Using the results

Research you can act on

Every study ends with clear conclusions and what they mean for your project: the right size and mix of retail, the tenants to target, or whether a location is worth taking.

Our team also works on strategy and leasing, so the research is written to be used.

Questions

Frequently asked questions

What does retail market research in Hong Kong usually cover?

Retail market research usually covers the catchment and its spending power, shopper behaviour, competing centres and streets, rents and vacancy. The exact mix depends on the decision behind it, such as a new development, a repositioning or a brand choosing a location.

Who uses retail market research and what for?

Mall owners use it to plan tenant mix and repositioning, developers to size and shape new retail projects, investors to understand a property's market, and retail brands to compare locations before signing a lease.

How long does a retail market study usually take?

It depends on the scope. A focused study on one location takes less time than a full market review that includes shopper surveys. We agree the scope, timeline and deliverables with you before work starts. [CONFIRM: typical durations]

Do you carry out retail market research outside Hong Kong?

Our base and most of our research work is in Hong Kong. Over more than two decades we have also worked on more than 100 retail projects across Asia Pacific and the Middle East, so we can support studies in other markets.

Can market research be combined with a feasibility study?

Yes. Market research is often the first part of a shopping mall feasibility study. The research shows what the market can support; the feasibility study then tests whether a specific project works on that basis.
