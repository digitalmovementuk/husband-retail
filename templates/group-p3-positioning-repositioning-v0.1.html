Every mall needs a reason for shoppers to choose it.

Positioning decides who a mall is for and why they come. These four steps are how we help owners set it, or reset it.

Positioning

Know what the mall stands for

A mall's position is its answer to three questions: who it serves, what it offers that others nearby do not, and how it should feel to visit. We work through those questions with the owner, using catchment, shopper and competitor evidence.

A clear position makes every later decision simpler, from which brands to approach to how the common areas are used.

Repositioning

When a mall needs a new direction

Malls age, shopping habits change and new centres open nearby. When footfall or rents start to slip, repositioning resets the offer around today's shoppers.

We plan the change in phases so the mall keeps trading, and we show the owner the expected effect of each phase before work begins.

Tenant mix

A mix shoppers come back for

The tenant mix is where a position becomes real. We look at which categories are missing, which are over-supplied and where tenants sit in relation to each other and to the main flows of shoppers.

The aim is a mall where each visit has a reason, and where tenants benefit from their neighbours.

Leasing

Repositioning that gets leased

A new direction works once the right tenants sign. Leasing has been part of our work since 1998, so every repositioning plan comes with target brands, zoning and a realistic leasing timeline.

Owners can then ask the same team to carry the plan through to agency leasing, keeping strategy and delivery in one place.

Questions

Frequently asked questions

What does a retail consulting firm do for a shopping mall?

A retail consulting firm helps a mall owner decide what the mall should offer and to whom, then plans the tenant mix, layout and leasing to get there. For an existing mall this often means repositioning; for a new one it means setting the position before leasing starts.

How do we know if our shopping mall needs repositioning?

Common signs are falling footfall, rising vacancy, tenants leaving at renewal, rents under pressure or a new competing centre nearby. A review of the tenant mix, shopper data and the local market shows whether small changes are enough or a wider repositioning makes sense.

Can a shopping mall be repositioned while it stays open?

Yes, in most cases. We plan repositioning in phases, so parts of the mall keep trading while others change. Timing follows lease expiries, fit-out works and key trading periods to protect income during the change.

Do you work with luxury malls and premium shopping centres?

Yes. Luxury and premium retail is part of our practice, including the positioning and repositioning of luxury malls. These projects need particular care with brand neighbours, space quality and the shopper experience. [CONFIRM: luxury project examples cleared for use]

What happens once the mall strategy has been agreed?

The strategy becomes a working plan: target tenants, zoning, any physical changes and a leasing timeline. Many owners then ask our team to lead the leasing as well, so the same people who set the direction see it through.
