Overview

Evidence for retail property decisions

Retail market research helps developers test the offer for a new destination and gives owners of established places evidence for the next phase. Each study starts from the decisions the client needs to make.

New destinations
We assess customer demand, competition and retailer requirements while the proposition and plan can still change.
Established destinations
We review the current offer against customer, tenant, market and commercial evidence and set priorities for the next phase.

Focus areas

Areas we advise on

The brief sets which research inputs are needed and how each one informs the next decision.

Market and demand analysis
Macro-economic conditions and the demand relevant to the destination and its proposed offer.
Consumer research
The needs and behaviour of the customers the destination intends to serve.
Competitor studies and benchmarks
Competing destinations and comparable schemes, as context for differentiation and the scale of the opportunity.
Brand interviews
Retailer intentions, requirements and expectations, gathered from the brands themselves.
Footfall and sales projections
Projections that support an assessment of supportable retail scale for the destination.
Positioning and concepts
Positioning statements and concept proposals tested against demand, competition and retailer requirements.
Tenant mix and target brands
Mix matrices and target-brand recommendations reviewed against the research findings.
Space requirements
Annotated plans on shop sizes, adjacencies and technical provisions for the design team.
Priorities and next steps
A roadmap of priorities, the further evidence needed and the next decisions.

Projections and their implications depend on the evidence and assumptions used. Design review provides commercial input; architectural and engineering responsibility stays with the specialists the client appoints.

Our approach

From research brief to agreed priorities

Each stage ends in an output the client can review. Timing and sequence depend on the brief, the evidence available and the project stage.

  1. 01

    Stage 1: Research questions

    We agree the commercial objectives with the client, the decisions the research must support and the questions it must answer.

    Deliverable:
    Agreed research brief
  2. 02

    Stage 2: Evidence and analysis

    We review customer, market and retailer information alongside the proposition and physical plan, and identify assumptions to test before a decision.

    Deliverable:
    Research findings
  3. 03

    Stage 3: Findings and implications

    We discuss the findings with the client and weigh their implications for positioning, new retail concepts, tenant mix and brand opportunities.

    Deliverable:
    Concept and mix recommendations
  4. 04

    Stage 4: Next decisions

    We set out priorities, responsibilities and the questions still open, including any brand engagement or leasing that follows the research.

    Deliverable:
    Roadmap of next steps

FAQ

Common questions on retail market research

What does a retail market report contribute to a project?

A retail market report can provide context for a market or sector. Research within a property appointment connects evidence with the project’s own decisions. The brief determines the outputs, depth of analysis and recommendations required.

Does research include brand engagement or leasing?

Brand engagement and leasing are included only where appointed. Research can conclude with findings, recommendations and agreed next steps. A subsequent mandate, including any role as exclusive leasing partner, is defined separately.
