Give customers a reason to choose your destination

A retail destination needs an offer that reflects its customers and gives them reasons to return. As shopping habits and competition change, that offer may need to change too. We help owners define the audience they want to serve and translate that positioning strategy into decisions about the property. For an established destination, the work connects its existing character with a renewed commercial direction.

The strategy gives the project team a shared basis for deciding:

  • Which customers the destination should attract and retain
  • What role it can occupy in its local market
  • How the tenant mix and layout should support that role
  • How marketing should communicate the offer and support visits

When to reassess your retail positioning

An operating destination may face new competition, changing visitor expectations or a retail offer that no longer reflects its surroundings. Repositioning provides a basis for reviewing those changes before committing to a new mix, refurbishment or relaunch.

The questions may include:

  • Does the current offer match the customers we want to reach?
  • Are some streets, levels or areas missing a clear purpose?
  • Which tenants and uses would support a stronger proposition?
  • Does the marketing reflect what visitors can experience?

Connect the positioning with decisions on the ground

Positioning needs to guide the choices made by the owner, design, leasing and marketing teams. We bring the commercial brief into those discussions so changes to the offer and the physical setting support the same direction.

What our retail repositioning strategy delivers

The scope reflects the property, its operating context and the decisions ahead. Our work can cover four connected areas.

01 Market research and positioning

Market research establishes the customer and competitive context. We use the findings to define the destination’s intended position and the retail offer that can support it. This gives the owner a commercial basis for assessing changes to an existing property or estate.

02 Retail mix and anchor strategy

The tenant mix translates the positioning into categories, brands and uses. An anchor strategy considers which operators can help establish the destination’s identity.

New-to-market brands may form part of the recommendations where they fit the audience and the wider offer.

03 Layout and zoning

Broad zoning sets out how the retail offer should be organised. For an urban estate, this can extend to layout and zoning by street. The retail programme brings the proposed mix and spatial recommendations together, with design review input where the appointment includes it.

04 Marketing plan and monitoring

A marketing plan connects the destination’s proposition with the customers it intends to attract. Monitoring the marketing and tenant-mix execution helps the owner review how the agreed direction is being carried through. The scope can include coordination around tenant participation and the evolving offer.

Together, these outputs connect the positioning with the work needed to put it into practice.

Build the offer around reasons to visit

A destination may serve office workers during the week and visitors seeking dining or leisure at other times. Those patterns should inform the balance of retail, dining and entertainment (RDE), the placement of anchors and the connections between streets or levels.

Luxury positioning needs the same discipline. The brands, setting and experience should fit the intended customer and the character of the place. Our luxury retail consultancy addresses that property context, connecting the proposition with mix, zoning and marketing decisions.

Questions about positioning and repositioning

What is a retail repositioning strategy?

A retail repositioning strategy defines how an existing destination should respond to its market. It connects customer and competitor research with a revised proposition, retail mix, layout and marketing direction. The aim is to give the owner a clear basis for decisions about the property.

How does positioning differ from repositioning?

Positioning defines the audience a destination will serve and its intended role in the market. Repositioning revisits that role for an existing property. It considers what should be retained, what needs to change and how the revised offer can be carried into practice.

Can you advise on luxury mall repositioning?

Yes. A luxury mall repositioning consultant needs to connect the intended customer with the property’s offer and setting. Our work on Hongkong Land’s Central portfolio and premium destinations such as Covent Garden provides relevant experience in retail mix, positioning and marketing.

Does repositioning require a complete refurbishment?

The strategy should establish which changes the destination needs. These may concern the mix, zoning or marketing, with physical changes considered against the brief. A design review can help the team assess how the layout supports the intended offer where this forms part of the appointment.

Does the service include leasing execution?

Leasing scope is agreed separately. Husband Retail undertakes leasing strategy and execution, including appointments as exclusive leasing partner. A repositioning strategy can inform tenant targeting, but introductions and lease negotiations need to be included in the agreed leasing mandate.

How is the repositioning carried through after planning?

The appointment can include monitoring the marketing plan and tenant-mix execution. This gives the owner a way to review implementation against the agreed proposition. Monitoring responsibilities, reporting arrangements and any performance measures should be agreed for the individual project.
