Development consultancy grounded in customer demand

Planning a retail destination starts with understanding its customers and the role it can play in its market. These insights shape the retail offer and inform decisions about the scale, character and organisation of the space. Our retail strategy consulting brings consumer research, positioning and planning together to guide those decisions. Working alongside developers and architects, we translate the findings into recommendations for tenant mix, zoning and layout. As a result, the development team has a shared basis for deciding:

  • Which customers and needs the destination will serve
  • How much retail space the market can support
  • Which anchors and uses belong in the mix
  • How tenant requirements should inform the design

When to bring retail strategy into development planning

Because plans are still open to change, early development advice can inform the scale, layout and mix. Our development advisory also supports schemes already in design, where a review can identify areas to refine. For example, a new site, a changing brief or an emerging design may raise questions that need a clearer commercial basis:

  • Is there demand for the proposed retail offer?
  • Does the scale reflect the market opportunity?
  • Will the layout suit the intended tenants?
  • How should retail connect with the wider development?

What our retail strategy consulting delivers

Because each project starts at a different stage, the scope reflects the decisions ahead. Our retail development consulting can include four areas of work.

01 — Market and feasibility assessment

Market and competition analysis, supported by consumer research, establishes the demand assumptions behind the scheme. For example, footfall and sales projections can help assess supportable retail scale. Rental projections then help the team consider the income implications of the proposed offer.

02 — Positioning and retail strategy

A positioning strategy defines the destination's intended place in its market. It therefore guides the concepts, theming and anchor strategy that give the retail offer a coherent direction. Recommendations can also include new-to-market brands where these fit customer demand.

03 — Tenant mix and retail programme

The retail programme then translates the strategy into a proposed tenant mix, broad zoning and area allocations. It also considers unit sizes and adjacencies, giving the team a clearer view of how the different parts of the destination should relate.

04 — Design brief and reviews

A design brief for the interior architect sets out the retail requirements. We also conduct design reviews with the architect, examining layout, circulation and unit configuration. This supports design and layout optimisation as the plans develop. In this way, the outputs connect the market assessment with practical planning decisions.

Retail strategy in practice

Our project work shows how research, positioning and design advice come together in different development settings.

Taipei Twin Towers, Taiwan

For Taipei Twin Towers Limited, we examined demand, competition and consumer behaviour for a destination connected to Taipei Main Station and the airport MRT. The work covered supportable scale, positioning, trade mix, zoning and potential brand mix. It also included a detailed design brief for the interior architect.

Seoul IFC, South Korea

Market research informed recommendations for the retail concept, layout and circulation. Our work also included retail unit design reviews and a retailer configuration plan to support leasing. In addition, the appointment covered leasing marketing tools and advice for tenant discussions.

Sanya Central Business District, China

For Gree Real Estate, consumer, retailer and competitor studies informed the project's positioning and retail offer. We then developed concepts and brand mixes for individual areas, alongside unit-level tenant recommendations. The work also covered theming, anchor strategy and broad zoning for the destination.

From retail strategy to programme execution

Our four-stage approach takes retail strategy consulting from feasibility through to support for implementation, with the scope agreed for each project.

  1. 01 — Feasibility and optimisation

    First, we review the development objectives, proposed scheme and commercial assumptions to identify the questions the study needs to resolve.

  2. 02 — Market research and analysis

    Next, we examine customers, competition and demand drivers. The findings help establish the market opportunity and test the assumptions behind the proposed retail offer.

  3. 03 — Develop the retail programme

    Then, we translate the research into positioning, concepts, mix and planning recommendations. The programme therefore gives the design team the commercial requirements to work with.

  4. 04 — Support programme execution

    We work with the project team as the programme moves into design and delivery. Design reviews also help carry the retail recommendations through subsequent decisions.

Plan around how people will use the place

For example, a commuter choosing lunch, an office worker meeting colleagues and a family spending an afternoon have different needs. Their reasons to visit should therefore influence the balance of retail, dining and entertainment (RDE), the location of anchors and how the space connects. The retail offer and the physical plan need to develop together. For mixed-use developments, this also means considering how shops and restaurants serve neighbouring offices, homes, hotels and public spaces. Research therefore gives those relationships a clearer basis.

Questions about development consultancy

What does a shopping mall feasibility study cover?

A shopping mall feasibility study examines customer demand, competition and the scale of retail that the market may support. Depending on the brief, it can also include footfall, sales and rental projections. These therefore help the developer assess the proposal against stated assumptions.

How does retail strategy consulting support a masterplan?

Retail master planning brings the intended customers, tenant mix and retail area requirements into the wider development plan. For example, we advise on retail positioning, broad zoning, connections and circulation alongside the architects. In this way, the retail component can relate to the other uses within the scheme.

Can you review the retail strategy for an existing design?

Yes. Design reviews can examine the proposed retail mix, unit sizes, adjacencies and customer routes against the intended positioning. However, the scope depends on how far the design has progressed and which decisions remain open. The review then identifies recommendations for the development team to consider.

How does transport connectivity shape the retail strategy?

Transport connections bring different visitor groups, journeys and time pressures. For example, at the airport-linked 11 Skies project in Hong Kong, our development advisory for New World Development included market and consumer research. It also examined phasing and the relationships between retail, dining and entertainment.

Can rental income be projected before leasing begins?

Rental projections can form part of the appointment, drawing on the proposed mix and the assumptions used for the scheme. They therefore help the developer assess potential rental income while planning is underway. However, actual income depends on subsequent leasing and trading conditions.

Does development consultancy include leasing?

We also undertake leasing strategy and execution, including appointments as exclusive leasing partner. However, the scope depends on the mandate. Development consultancy establishes recommendations that can inform leasing, while tenant approaches and lease negotiations form part of the agreed leasing appointment.
